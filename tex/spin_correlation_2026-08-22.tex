\begin{abstract}
This report reconstructs the validated research progress for 22 August 2026 after the automatically
generated daily artifact failed to persist. The strongest result of the day is a microscopic
selection rule for the STAR scalar spin-correlation observable:
a broad class of exchange-symmetric coherent two-spin Hamiltonians cannot change
$\Tr C=\langle\bm\sigma_1\!\cdot\!\bm\sigma_2\rangle$.
Therefore the measured loss of $\Lambda\bar\Lambda$ correlation requires
singlet--triplet conversion driven by \emph{relative} spin fields, exchange-antisymmetric interactions,
or genuinely dissipative/collisional terms. This result provides a sharper target for a future
two-particle Wigner/Kadanoff--Baym derivation. We also review how this result interfaces with
the ${}^3P_0$ source, feed-down, the STAR observable, and existing quantum-kinetic literature.
\end{abstract}

\section{Status and scope}

The long-term objective is to construct a calculational chain
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\rho_{\ssb}^{\rm source}
\longrightarrow
\text{microscopic spin transport}
\longrightarrow
\text{hadronization / feed-down}
\longrightarrow
\rho_{\LLb}^{\rm obs}
}
\end{equation}
that explains the measured $\LLb$ spin correlation and its dependence on pair separation.

The STAR measurement in $pp$ collisions reports a short-range relative polarization
\begin{equation}
P_{\LLb}^{\rm STAR}
=
0.181\pm0.035_{\rm stat}\pm0.022_{\rm sys},
\label{eq:starvalue}
\end{equation}
with the correlation decreasing substantially at larger angular separation.
The experimental observable is naturally written in terms of the two-spin correlation tensor
$C_{ij}$,
\begin{equation}
\rho
=
\frac14
\left[
\Id\otimes\Id
+P_i\sigma_i\otimes\Id
+\bar P_j\Id\otimes\sigma_j
+C_{ij}\sigma_i\otimes\sigma_j
\right].
\label{eq:rho}
\end{equation}
For the rotationally averaged scalar used by STAR,
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
P_{\LLb}
=
\frac13\Trc C
=
\frac13
\left\langle
\bm\sigma_1\cdot\bm\sigma_2
\right\rangle .
}
\label{eq:scalar}
\end{equation}

Equation~\eqref{eq:scalar} is the central quantity whose microscopic time evolution we studied on
22 August.

\section{Source state: what remains robust}

A vacuum-like pair-creation mechanism with $J^{PC}=0^{++}$ naturally leads to a ${}^3P_0$ state,
\begin{equation}
L=1,\qquad S=1,\qquad J=0.
\end{equation}
The exact coupled state is
\begin{equation}
|J=0,M=0\rangle
=
\frac{1}{\sqrt3}
\sum_{m=-1}^{1}
(-1)^{1-m}
|L=1,m\rangle
|S=1,-m\rangle .
\label{eq:3p0}
\end{equation}

If the orbital magnetic quantum number is completely unobserved, tracing over orbital space gives
\begin{align}
\rho_S
&=
\Trc_L |J=0\rangle\langle J=0|\\
&=
\frac13
\sum_{m=-1}^{1}
|1,m\rangle\langle1,m|\\
&=
\frac13 P_{S=1}.
\end{align}
For two spin-$1/2$ particles,
\begin{equation}
P_{S=1}
=
\frac{3\Id+\bm\sigma_1\cdot\bm\sigma_2}{4},
\end{equation}
so
\begin{equation}
\rho_S
=
\frac14
\left[
\Id\otimes\Id
+
\frac13
\sum_i\sigma_i\otimes\sigma_i
\right].
\end{equation}
Thus
\begin{equation}
C_{ij}^{\rm avg}
=
\frac13\delta_{ij},
\qquad
P_{\ssb}^{(0)}
=
\frac13.
\label{eq:p13}
\end{equation}

The distinction between this orbitally averaged state and a momentum-conditioned local spin state
remains important. A future event-by-event theory should keep the orbital/fragmentation axis until
the experimental projection is made.

\section{Exact coherent two-spin evolution}

\subsection{Most general bilinear Hamiltonian}

We consider
\begin{equation}
H=
H_1+H_2+H_{12},
\end{equation}
with
\begin{align}
H_1&=
\frac12\Omega_a\,\sigma_a\otimes\Id,\\
H_2&=
\frac12\bar\Omega_b\,\Id\otimes\sigma_b,\\
H_{12}
&=
\frac14J_{ab}\,\sigma_a\otimes\sigma_b.
\label{eq:H}
\end{align}
Here $\bm\Omega$ and $\bar{\bm\Omega}$ are effective local precession frequencies for the
$s$ and $\bar s$, while $J_{ab}$ is the most general real bilinear spin-coupling tensor compatible
with Hermiticity.

The density matrix evolves exactly according to
\begin{equation}
\dot\rho=-i[H,\rho].
\label{eq:vonNeumann}
\end{equation}
No Markov approximation or master-equation ansatz is used at this stage.

The correlation tensor is
\begin{equation}
C_{ij}
=
\Trc\left[
\rho\,
\sigma_i\otimes\sigma_j
\right].
\end{equation}
Differentiating,
\begin{align}
\dot C_{ij}
&=
\Trc\left[
\dot\rho\,
\sigma_i\otimes\sigma_j
\right]\\
&=
-i\Trc\left[
[H,\rho]\,
\sigma_i\otimes\sigma_j
\right].
\end{align}
Using cyclicity of the trace,
\begin{equation}
\Trc([H,\rho]O)
=
\Trc(\rho[O,H]),
\end{equation}
we obtain
\begin{equation}
\dot C_{ij}
=
-i\left\langle
[\sigma_i\otimes\sigma_j,H]
\right\rangle.
\label{eq:Ccomm}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Local-field contribution}

Using
\begin{equation}
[\sigma_i,\sigma_a]=2i\eps_{iak}\sigma_k,
\end{equation}
the first-spin term gives
\begin{align}
-i\left[
\sigma_i\otimes\sigma_j,
\frac12\Omega_a\sigma_a\otimes\Id
\right]
&=
-i\frac{\Omega_a}{2}
[\sigma_i,\sigma_a]\otimes\sigma_j\\
&=
\Omega_a\eps_{iak}\,
\sigma_k\otimes\sigma_j.
\end{align}
Therefore
\begin{equation}
\left.\dot C_{ij}\right|_{1}
=
\eps_{iak}\Omega_a C_{kj}.
\end{equation}
Similarly,
\begin{equation}
\left.\dot C_{ij}\right|_{2}
=
\eps_{jbl}\bar\Omega_b C_{il}.
\end{equation}

\subsection{Bilinear interaction contribution}

For the interaction term,
\begin{equation}
[\sigma_i\otimes\sigma_j,\sigma_a\otimes\sigma_b]
=
[\sigma_i,\sigma_a]\otimes
\frac{\{\sigma_j,\sigma_b\}}2
+
\frac{\{\sigma_i,\sigma_a\}}2
\otimes[\sigma_j,\sigma_b].
\end{equation}
Since
\begin{equation}
\{\sigma_i,\sigma_a\}=2\delta_{ia}\Id,
\end{equation}
we find
\begin{align}
[\sigma_i\otimes\sigma_j,\sigma_a\otimes\sigma_b]
&=
2i\eps_{iak}\delta_{jb}\,
\sigma_k\otimes\Id\\
&\quad+
2i\delta_{ia}\eps_{jbl}\,
\Id\otimes\sigma_l.
\end{align}
Multiplying by $J_{ab}/4$ and by $-i$ gives
\begin{equation}
\left.\dot C_{ij}\right|_{12}
=
\frac12\eps_{iak}J_{aj}P_k
+
\frac12\eps_{jbl}J_{ib}\bar P_l.
\end{equation}

Combining all pieces,
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
\dot C_{ij}
={}&
\eps_{iak}\Omega_aC_{kj}
+
\eps_{jbl}\bar\Omega_bC_{il}\\
&+
\frac12\eps_{iak}J_{aj}P_k
+
\frac12\eps_{jbl}J_{ib}\bar P_l .
\end{aligned}}
\label{eq:fullC}
\end{equation}

This is an exact coherent evolution equation for the complete tensor.

\section{Protection theorem for the STAR scalar}

Define
\begin{equation}
Q
\equiv
\bm\sigma_1\cdot\bm\sigma_2
=
\sum_i\sigma_i\otimes\sigma_i.
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation}
3P=\langle Q\rangle.
\end{equation}

Introduce the spin-exchange operator
\begin{equation}
\mathsf P_{12}
=
\frac12(\Id+Q),
\label{eq:swap}
\end{equation}
so
\begin{equation}
Q=2\mathsf P_{12}-\Id.
\end{equation}
Therefore
\begin{equation}
[H,Q]=2[H,\mathsf P_{12}].
\end{equation}

If the Hamiltonian is invariant under spin exchange,
\begin{equation}
\mathsf P_{12}H\mathsf P_{12}=H,
\end{equation}
then
\begin{equation}
[H,\mathsf P_{12}]=0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
[H,Q]=0.
\end{equation}
Hence
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\frac{d}{dt}\langle Q\rangle=0
}
\end{equation}
and therefore
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\frac{d}{dt}\Trc C=0.
}
\label{eq:protect}
\end{equation}

For the Hamiltonian in Eq.~\eqref{eq:H}, exchange invariance requires
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\bm\Omega=\bar{\bm\Omega},
\qquad
J_{ij}=J_{ji}.
}
\label{eq:conds}
\end{equation}

Thus any common coherent precession and any exchange-symmetric bilinear interaction can rotate
or redistribute tensor components but cannot change the scalar measured by STAR.

This includes, in particular,
\begin{equation}
H_{\rm ex}
=
J(r)\,\bm S_1\cdot\bm S_2,
\end{equation}
as well as symmetric anisotropic couplings
\begin{equation}
H_{\rm sym}
=
S_1^iJ^{(S)}_{ij}S_2^j,
\qquad
J^{(S)}_{ij}=J^{(S)}_{ji}.
\end{equation}

\section{Singlet probability: a sharper physical interpretation}

For two spin-$1/2$ particles the singlet projector is
\begin{equation}
\Pi_S
=
|S\rangle\langle S|
=
\frac14
\left(
\Id-\bm\sigma_1\cdot\bm\sigma_2
\right).
\end{equation}
Therefore
\begin{align}
p_S
&=
\Trc(\rho\Pi_S)\\
&=
\frac14
\left[
1-\langle Q\rangle
\right]\\
&=
\boxed{
\frac{1-3P}{4}
}.
\label{eq:pS}
\end{align}

This relation reframes the problem.

If the initial source is purely triplet,
\begin{equation}
p_S(0)=0,
\qquad
P(0)=\frac13.
\end{equation}
A reduction in $P$ corresponds to a growth of singlet weight.

Using the STAR central value only as a hadron-level diagnostic,
\begin{equation}
p_S^{\rm obs}
=
\frac{1-3(0.181)}{4}
\simeq 0.114.
\end{equation}
This number is not yet the primordial partonic singlet probability because hadronization and
feed-down have not been unfolded. Its value simply illustrates the physical content of the measured
scalar.

The key microscopic question therefore becomes
\begin{quote}
Which QCD interactions can transfer probability between the triplet and singlet sectors?
\end{quote}

\section{Which coherent terms can mix singlet and triplet?}

Decompose the local frequencies as
\begin{equation}
\bm\Omega_c
=
\frac{\bm\Omega+\bar{\bm\Omega}}{2},
\qquad
\delta\bm\Omega
=
\frac{\bm\Omega-\bar{\bm\Omega}}{2}.
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{align}
H_{\rm local}
&=
\bm\Omega_c\cdot(\bm S_1+\bm S_2)
+
\delta\bm\Omega\cdot(\bm S_1-\bm S_2).
\end{align}
The first term is exchange symmetric; the second is exchange antisymmetric.

For a differential field along $z$,
\begin{equation}
H_\delta
=
\frac{\delta\Omega_z}{2}
(\sigma_{1z}-\sigma_{2z}).
\end{equation}
Using
\begin{align}
|T_0\rangle
&=
\frac{
|\uparrow\downarrow\rangle+
|\downarrow\uparrow\rangle
}{\sqrt2},\\
|S\rangle
&=
\frac{
|\uparrow\downarrow\rangle-
|\downarrow\uparrow\rangle
}{\sqrt2},
\end{align}
we obtain
\begin{align}
(\sigma_{1z}-\sigma_{2z})|\uparrow\downarrow\rangle
&=2|\uparrow\downarrow\rangle,\\
(\sigma_{1z}-\sigma_{2z})|\downarrow\uparrow\rangle
&=-2|\downarrow\uparrow\rangle.
\end{align}
Therefore
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
(\sigma_{1z}-\sigma_{2z})|T_0\rangle
=
2|S\rangle.
}
\end{equation}

The two-state Hamiltonian in the $\{|T_0\rangle,|S\rangle\}$ basis is proportional to
\begin{equation}
H_\delta
\sim
\delta\Omega_z
\begin{pmatrix}
0&1\\
1&0
\end{pmatrix}.
\end{equation}
Starting from $|T_0\rangle$,
\begin{equation}
p_S(t)=\sin^2(\delta\Omega_z t),
\end{equation}
and since
\begin{equation}
P=\frac{1-4p_S}{3},
\end{equation}
we obtain
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
P(t)
=
\frac13
\left[
1-4\sin^2(\delta\Omega_z t)
\right].
}
\label{eq:osc}
\end{equation}

Hence \emph{relative} spin fields are capable of coherent singlet--triplet conversion.

\subsection{Exchange-antisymmetric bilinear interaction}

Decompose
\begin{equation}
J_{ij}
=
J^{(S)}_{ij}
+
J^{(A)}_{ij},
\end{equation}
with
\begin{equation}
J^{(A)}_{ij}
=
\frac12(J_{ij}-J_{ji}).
\end{equation}
Any antisymmetric rank-two tensor can be written as
\begin{equation}
J^{(A)}_{ij}
=
\eps_{ijk}D_k.
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{align}
H_A
&=
S_1^iJ^{(A)}_{ij}S_2^j\\
&=
D_k\eps_{ijk}S_1^iS_2^j\\
&=
\boxed{
\bm D\cdot(\bm S_1\times\bm S_2)
}.
\end{align}
This Dzyaloshinskii--Moriya-like term is odd under exchange and can also mix the singlet and triplet
sectors.

\section{Implication for the kinetic theory}

The coherent calculation suggests that the most economical coarse-grained variable for the scalar
measurement is the singlet probability rather than a generic ``decoherence parameter.''

A minimal kinetic description is
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\dot p_S
=
\Gamma_{T\rightarrow S}p_T
-
\Gamma_{S\rightarrow T}p_S
+
S_S^{\rm prod},
}
\label{eq:rate}
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
p_T=1-p_S
\end{equation}
if leakage outside the two-spin Hilbert space is neglected.

Using Eq.~\eqref{eq:pS},
\begin{equation}
P=\frac{1-4p_S}{3},
\end{equation}
so
\begin{align}
\dot P
&=
-\frac43\dot p_S\\
&=
-\frac43
\left[
\Gamma_{T\to S}(1-p_S)
-\Gamma_{S\to T}p_S
+S_S^{\rm prod}
\right].
\end{align}
This explicitly separates singlet--triplet conversion from source preparation.

The central microscopic task is now to derive
\begin{equation}
\Gamma_{T\to S},
\qquad
\Gamma_{S\to T}
\end{equation}
from a two-particle nonequilibrium QCD kernel.

\section{Connection to the Wigner/Kadanoff--Baym route}

Relativistic quantum kinetic theory for massive quarks starts with a gauge-covariant Wigner function,
which can be decomposed in the Clifford basis as
\begin{equation}
W
=
\frac14
\left[
\mathcal F
+i\gamma^5\mathcal P
+\gamma^\mu\mathcal V_\mu
+\gamma^\mu\gamma^5\mathcal A_\mu
+\frac12\sigma^{\mu\nu}\mathcal S_{\mu\nu}
\right].
\end{equation}
The axial component $\mathcal A^\mu$ carries spin information.

The one-body quantum kinetic literature derives color-singlet and color-octet equations for
$\mathcal V^\mu$ and $\mathcal A^\mu$, including diffusion and color-field-correlation source terms.
This provides the correct relativistic starting point for strange-quark spin transport.

For the present problem, however, we require the two-particle quantity
\begin{equation}
W^{(2)}(1,2)
=
\left\langle
\widehat W_s(1)\otimes
\widehat W_{\bar s}(2)
\right\rangle.
\end{equation}
The target connected axial-axial correlator is schematically
\begin{equation}
\mathcal C^{\mu\nu}(1,2)
=
\left\langle
\mathcal A_s^\mu(1)
\mathcal A_{\bar s}^\nu(2)
\right\rangle_c.
\end{equation}

Projecting this correlator onto singlet/triplet projectors should yield the microscopic gain/loss
terms in Eq.~\eqref{eq:rate}. In a Kadanoff--Baym formulation, these rates must arise from
self-energy/collision kernels that are odd under relative-spin exchange or otherwise distinguish the
two spin histories.

\section{Relation to existing literature}

The STAR observation establishes a short-range positive $\Lambda\bar\Lambda$ correlation and a
substantial reduction with angular separation. The nonrelativistic SU(6) interpretation assumes the
$\Lambda$ spin is carried by the strange quark, motivating the use of hyperon spin as a probe of
ancestral strange-quark spin.

Barata et al.\ model the correlation loss with a spatially correlated depolarizing open-system
description. Their result that relative environmental noise controls the decay of the scalar is
consistent with the algebraic protection theorem above: common-mode spin rotations preserve
$\bm\sigma_1\cdot\bm\sigma_2$.

Yang's quantum kinetic theory derives massive-quark spin transport from the Wigner-function approach
and obtains spin-diffusion and source terms determined by color-field correlators. It therefore
supplies a first-principles language for replacing phenomenological spin-noise rates.

Kumar, M\"uller and Yang further show that glasma color fields can generate nonzero
quark--antiquark spin correlations even when single-particle polarization vanishes. Such a mechanism
belongs to the production/source sector and should not be conflated with later singlet--triplet
decoherence.

\section{Hadronization and feed-down remain indispensable}

Even after the partonic singlet--triplet kinetics is derived, the observable tensor is
\begin{equation}
C^{\Lambda\bar\Lambda}_{ij}
=
\sum_{ab}
R_{ab}\,
(A_a)_{ik}\,
C^{s\bar s,(ab)}_{kl}\,
(\bar A_b)_{jl},
\label{eq:feed}
\end{equation}
where $a,b$ label production/feed-down channels and $A_a,\bar A_b$ are channel-specific spin-transfer
maps.

The key lesson from earlier cycles remains valid:
feed-down cannot be represented safely by a single universal scalar dilution if different channels
have different tensor transfer coefficients.

This means that the singlet fraction inferred directly from Eq.~\eqref{eq:starvalue} is only an
observable-level diagnostic. A quantitative partonic interpretation requires Eq.~\eqref{eq:feed}.

\section{What was genuinely advanced on 22 August}

The main advances of the day can be stated compactly.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item The full coherent tensor equation, Eq.~\eqref{eq:fullC}, was derived directly from

\section{Highest-value next derivation}

The next calculation should start from a two-particle Schwinger--Keldysh Green function
\begin{equation}
G^{<(2)}(1,2;1',2')
\end{equation}
and derive coupled equations acting on the $s$ and $\bar s$ legs.

After Wigner transformation and a controlled gradient/quasiparticle expansion, the collision kernel
should be projected with
\begin{equation}
\Pi_S
=
\frac14
(\Id-\bm\sigma_1\cdot\bm\sigma_2)
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
\Pi_T=\Id-\Pi_S.
\end{equation}
The immediate target is
\begin{equation}
\boxed{
\Gamma_{T\to S}
\sim
\Trc\!\left[
\Pi_S\,\mathcal C_{\rm coll}[\Pi_T]
\right],
\qquad
\Gamma_{S\to T}
\sim
\Trc\!\left[
\Pi_T\,\mathcal C_{\rm coll}[\Pi_S]
\right].
}
\end{equation}

Once these rates are obtained from QCD field correlators or a specified microscopic model, the
measured $\Delta R$ dependence can be interpreted as an actual singlet--triplet conversion history
rather than a generic phenomenological loss factor.
